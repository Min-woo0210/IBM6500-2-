## Research Question

The questionnaire asks respondents to select all applicable reasons for joining. This is a multiple-response categorical question. The correct analysis is a frequency and percentage summary for each reason, followed by a bar chart ranking the reasons.

R2: Descriptive Multiple-Response Frequencies - Why Do Members Borrow Elsewhere?

Research Question

R2: Why do members use other financial institutions when they need to borrow funds?

R2 requires Q28 to identify members who borrowed from another source and Q29 to identify the reasons. Because Q29 allows more than one reason, the appropriate method is a multiple-response frequency analysis among respondents who answered yes to Q28.

Data Limitation

R5: Descriptive Frequencies and Percentages - Awareness of Credit Union Services

Research Question

R5: How well are members aware of the services offered by the credit union?

Q13–Q26 measure service awareness. The response categories are:

  1. Aware and have used
  2. Aware but have not used
  3. Unaware of the service

```{r} awareness_items <- case31 |> select(q13:q26)

awareness_summary <- awareness_items |> summarise(across(everything(), list( used = ~ mean(.x == 1, na.rm = TRUE), aware_not_used = ~ mean(.x == 2, na.rm = TRUE), unaware = ~ mean(.x == 3, na.rm = TRUE) ))) |> pivot_longer(everything(), names_to = “measure”, values_to = “percent”) |> mutate(percent = round(percent * 100, 1))

## Key Awareness Results

The service-awareness distribution is summarized in Table @tbl-r5-awareness. The main insight comes from the pattern of responses across services, not from a single test statistic.

Table: Service-awareness results for selected Case 31 services. {#tbl-r5-awareness}

Across the services, awareness and usage vary substantially. MasterCard and line-of-credit services have relatively high awareness, while regular share accounts, share-collateralized loans, and IRA loans have large unaware groups. Signature loans have high awareness but a very large aware-but-not-used group, suggesting that awareness alone does not guarantee adoption.

## Management Implication

The credit union should distinguish two communication problems: services with high unawareness require education, while services with high awareness but low usage require a value proposition, product explanation, or conversion strategy.

# R6: Descriptive Means - Attitudes About How the Credit Union Is Operated

## Research Question

**R6: What are the members' attitudes and beliefs about how effectively the credit union is operated?**

Q30–Q34 measure members' attitudes toward loan rates, confidentiality, loan processing, service adequacy, and application simplicity. Higher values indicate stronger agreement with each statement.

```{r}
operations <- case31 |>
  summarise(
    loan_rates_lower = mean(q30, na.rm = TRUE),
    personnel_confidential = mean(q31, na.rm = TRUE),
    loan_processing_prompt = mean(q32, na.rm = TRUE),
    services_meet_needs = mean(q33, na.rm = TRUE),
    loan_application_simple = mean(q34, na.rm = TRUE)
  ) |>
  pivot_longer(everything(), names_to = "item", values_to = "mean")

operations |>
  mutate(mean = round(mean, 2)) |>
  kable(col.names = c("Operational statement", "Mean"))

The strongest evaluation concerns confidentiality, followed by prompt loan processing. The weakest evaluation concerns whether credit union loan rates are lower than those offered by other institutions. The overall operations mean of 3.56 indicates a moderately positive evaluation, but the rate comparison is an area where the credit union may be less competitive or less clearly differentiated.
