Purpose and Case Context

This report assesses the measurement model for Case 31: Employees Federal Credit Union. The goal is to determine whether the survey items provide defensible measures of two latent constructs:

  1. Attitudes toward employee proficiencies, measured by Q3–Q6.
  2. Attitude toward operational effectiveness, measured by Q9r, Q10, Q11, Q12, and Q31–Q34 after documenting the direction of each item.

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) evaluates the measurement model. It does not test the substantive hypotheses in the Case 31 analysis table and does not establish causal relationships.

Learning Notes: CFA and Measurement Models

A latent construct cannot be observed directly. Instead, it is represented by multiple observed indicators that are expected to share common variance. CFA begins with a theory-based measurement model and evaluates whether the observed covariance pattern is consistent with that model.

The main questions in this report are:

  • Do the proposed indicators load substantially on their intended factors?
  • Does the overall model fit the observed data reasonably well?
  • Are the factors internally consistent and convergent?
  • Are employee proficiency and operational effectiveness empirically distinguishable?
  • Is the measurement model sufficiently defensible for later structural analysis?

Single-item variables from the Case 31 questionnaire are not modeled as latent factors because a single observed item does not provide information about internal consistency or a measurement-error structure in the same way as a multi-item factor.

Aligning item direction

The raw coding directions are not identical. Q10 is coded so that 1 means “Excellent” and 4 means “Poor”; Q11 uses 1 = “Yes” and 2 = “No”; and Q12 uses 1 = “Yes,” 2 = “No,” and 3 = “Uncertain.” To make larger values consistently represent more favorable operational evaluations, I use the existing q10_r and create explicit favorable-direction versions of Q11 and Q12.

```{r} case31_cfa <- case31 |> mutate( q9r_final = q9_r, q10r_final = q10_r, q11r_final = 3 - q11, q12r_final = 4 - q12 )

# Hypothesized Two-Factor Measurement Model

The two factors are expected to be related because members’ experiences with employees may influence their broader evaluation of credit-union operations. They are nevertheless specified as distinct factors because employee behavior and organizational operations are conceptually different domains.

The model is identified by fixing the first loading of each factor to 1.0 in `lavaan` by default. Each factor has multiple indicators, and the two factors are allowed to correlate.

```{r}
measurement_model <- '
  Employee_Proficiencies =~ q3 + q4 + q5 + q6
  Operational_Effectiveness =~ q9r_final + q10r_final + q11r_final + q12r_final +
    q31 + q32 + q33 + q34
'

fit <- cfa(
  measurement_model,
  data = case31_cfa,
  estimator = "ML",
  missing = "fiml",
  std.lv = FALSE
)

summary(fit, fit.measures = TRUE, standardized = TRUE)

The proposed two-factor model does not fit the data well. The chi-square test is significant, and the incremental fit indices are below common benchmarks such as .90 or .95. RMSEA is approximately .105 and SRMR is approximately .102, both suggesting substantial residual misfit. These results mean that the one-factor specification for operational effectiveness should not be accepted automatically.

Small numerical differences may occur when the code is re-rendered in a different R/lavaan version or with a robust estimator. The substantive conclusion is stable: the hypothesized model shows inadequate fit and requires measurement-model qualification.

## Discriminant validity

The estimated latent-factor correlation is approximately .70. This indicates that members’ views of employee proficiencies and operational effectiveness are related, but the correlation is not so high that the constructs are automatically identical. However, discriminant validity cannot be declared solely from the factor correlation because the operational factor has poor convergent evidence and poor overall model fit.

The strongest evidence for conceptual distinction is that Q3–Q6 form a strong, coherent employee-proficiency cluster, while the operational indicators are heterogeneous. The more important limitation is that the operational factor itself is not measured consistently enough to support a confident discriminant-validity claim. A future analysis should compare theoretically justified alternatives, such as separate operational subdimensions, rather than adding correlated errors only to improve fit.

```{r}
lavInspect(fit, "cor.lv")
modindices(fit, sort. = TRUE, minimum.value = 10) |>

Modification indices may be used diagnostically, but I do not add correlated errors or delete items in the primary model. Any alternative model would need a substantive explanation based on the questionnaire and would have to be labeled and compared with the original model.

Interpretation and Conclusion

The two-factor CFA provides strong evidence for the employee-proficiency construct. Q3–Q6 have strong standardized loadings, good composite reliability, and acceptable average variance extracted. These items can reasonably be treated as a latent employee-proficiency measure in a subsequent model.

The evidence for the operational-effectiveness construct is substantially weaker. The one-factor model fits poorly, most operational indicators have weak loadings, and CR and AVE are low. The operational items cover several different experiences—statement frequency, statement quality, confidentiality, processing speed, service adequacy, and application simplicity—which may not represent one unidimensional factor. The result is therefore a measurement concern rather than evidence that the credit union’s operations are objectively poor.

Before testing structural hypotheses, I would retain the employee-proficiency factor but investigate the operational indicators further. The next step could be to review the Case 31 measurement table and theory, test theoretically justified subdimensions, or analyze operational items separately. I would not use the current eight-item operational factor as an unquestioned composite or interpret the CFA as a causal test.
